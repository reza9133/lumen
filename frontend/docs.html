Documentation

Lumen

Public vows, judged by validators. A keeper writes a promise, stakes GEN behind it and points to the page that will prove it. After the deadline, every GenLayer validator reads that page and rules whether the vow was kept.

Keepers

Make a vow with a stake of at least 0.1 GEN and a link to the evidence page.

Faith and doubt

Anyone else can back a vow with faith, or bet against it with doubt, from 0.01 GEN.

Validators

Read the evidence, ask an LLM for a verdict and must agree on the verdict field.

What is in the repository

Key terms

Vow
A public promise of up to 280 characters, tied to a stake, an evidence link and a deadline.
Keeper
The address that made the vow and staked on it.
Faith
Backing that the vow will be kept. Total faith on a vow is capped at half of the keeper's stake.
Doubt
Backing that the vow will be broken. Doubt has no cap.
Evidence
The public web page the validators read to decide the vow.
Embers
GEN burned by broken vows. It stays in the contract and is never paid out. The app shows it as embers drifting down the sky.

Quick start

Lumen runs on GenLayer Studionet. You need Node 18 or newer, Python 3.12 or newer and a browser wallet.

1. Deploy the contract

pip install -r requirements.txt
genvm-lint check contracts/lumen.py

npm install -g genlayer
genlayer network set studionet
genlayer deploy --contract contracts/lumen.py

2. Start the app

Copy the printed contract address, then:

cd frontend
cp .env.example .env        # paste the address into VITE_LUMEN_ADDRESS
npm install
npm run dev

Or deploy from a script that also writes the address into frontend/.env:

cd frontend
npm install
DEPLOY_KEY=0x<funded private key> npm run deploy

3. Try the whole cycle

Open the app and connect your wallet; it is switched to Studionet automatically. Fund the address from the faucet in GenLayer Studio, then light a lantern with the five minute deadline to see a full cycle quickly.

Version pinning. genlayer-js is pinned to 1.1.8 and package-lock.json is committed. The 2.x line targets the Consensus v0.6 networks and changes how fees work, so move to it deliberately, together with the network it matches.
Missing runner. If Studionet reports a missing runner, replace the Depends hash on the first line of the contract with the one used by the example contracts in Studio.

Vow lifecycle

A vow moves through five states: OPEN (0), KEPT (1), BROKEN (2), UNCLEAR (3) and REVIEW (4). A kept or broken verdict is first only proposed: the vow sits in REVIEW while the losing side can dispute it, and nothing is payable until finalize makes it final. UNCLEAR is final at once.

  1. 1

    Make

    make_vow locks the keeper's stake. Minimum 0.1 GEN.

  2. 2

    Back

    Until the deadline, back adds faith or doubt. Doubters may also challenge.

  3. 3

    Judge

    After the deadline anyone calls judge. Validators read the evidence and agree on a proposed verdict. A favourable one needs a verified quote and date.

  4. 4

    Review and finalize

    The losing side can dispute during the review window. Then anyone calls finalize.

  5. 5

    Claim

    Each address calls claim once to withdraw what it is owed.

Optional steps around judging

Counter-evidence

Until the deadline, a doubter can call challenge(vow_id, url) once to point the judges at a page that argues the vow failed. The judges read it together with the keeper's page and treat it as a claim rather than a fact. It is used only when it gives concrete facts (dates, names, links, numbers) that contradict the keeper's page. Counter-evidence can lower a verdict, never raise one.

A vow keeps at most three such pages. When all slots are taken, a doubter whose doubt is larger than the smallest holder's current doubt replaces that holder, who may then submit again.

Pinned proof

Before the deadline the keeper can call pin_evidence(vow_id, url) up to twice to attach proof that cannot be rewritten: an exact Wayback capture (web.archive.org/web/<14 digits>/<url>, whose capture time must not be in the future) or a commit-pinned or content-addressed link. Validators read pins together with the keeper's page. Firm proof gets a shorter review window and cannot be outweighed by an editable counter-page.

Review window and disputes

A kept or broken proposal can be disputed with dispute(vow_id, url) until review_end: one eighth of the vow's lifetime, at least ten minutes and at most two days, doubled when the proof rests on an editable page and for every broken proposal. Against a kept proposal, doubters dispute with counter-evidence. Against a broken proposal, the keeper and faith backers dispute with a rebuttal page, which still has to show a dated completion on or before the deadline. Three slots, one page per address, a larger position replaces the smallest. After the window anyone calls finalize: without disputes the proposal stands; with disputes the validators read once more and that result is final. If the keeper's page cannot be read during a dispute of a kept proposal, the vow closes as unclear.

Evidence provenance

Every link is classed by its shape as snapshot, permalink or mutable. A FULFILLED answer must name a source, copy a verbatim quote of at least 12 characters and, unless the source is a snapshot (its date is in the link), copy the words that give the date plus a YYYY-MM-DD that they show. The contract verifies the quote, the date and that it is on or before the deadline; otherwise the verdict is BROKEN. Every validator also checks the leader's quote against its own copy.

Doubter's pageKeeper's proofResult
firmeditableBROKEN
firmfirmUNCLEAR
editableeditableUNCLEAR
editablefirmignored

Early confirmation

Halfway to the deadline, judge can be called while nobody doubts the vow. An early check can only confirm the vow, and a confirmation opens a review window during which the vow stays open to new doubt (not faith). If the page does not show the work yet, the vow stays open, a note is recorded and nothing is counted against the keeper. Early checks of one vow are at least fifteen minutes apart.

Release

If a vow has no final verdict thirty days after its deadline, or after the end of its review window (judging keeps failing, or nobody asked), anyone can call release. It closes the vow as unclear and everyone can claim a full refund.

Verdicts and payouts

VerdictKeeperFaith backersDoubters
KeptStake back, plus the doubt pool if nobody backed with faithBacking back, plus a pro-rata share of the doubt poolLose their backing
BrokenLoses the stakeLose their backingBacking back, plus a pro-rata share of half the stake and the whole faith pool
UnclearStake backBacking backBacking back

Half of a broken stake is burned. If nobody doubted a broken vow, its stake and faith pool are burned in full; the app warns faith backers about this before they back a vow nobody doubts. Burned GEN stays in the contract and is never paid out.

The formulas

With stake, faith and doubt as the vow's totals, and f and d as one address's own faith and doubt:

KEPT     keeper : stake  (+ doubt, if faith == 0)
         backer : f + doubt * f / faith

BROKEN   doubter: d + ((stake - stake/2) + faith) * d / doubt
         burned : stake/2          if doubt > 0
                  stake + faith    if doubt == 0

UNCLEAR  everyone: their own stake or backing, in full

All divisions are integer divisions in wei, so rounding dust stays at a few wei.

Worked example

A keeper stakes 1 GEN. Backer A puts 0.4 GEN on faith. Doubters D1 and D2 put 0.3 and 0.1 GEN on doubt. The contract holds 1.8 GEN.

Broken: the pot is (1 − 0.5) + 0.4 = 0.9 GEN. D1 gets 0.3 + 0.9 × 0.3 / 0.4 = 0.975 and D2 gets 0.1 + 0.9 × 0.1 / 0.4 = 0.325. Kept: A is the only faith backer, so A takes the whole doubt pool: 0.4 + 0.4 × 0.4 / 0.4 = 0.8.

Why half is burned, and why faith is capped

A keeper can doubt their own vow from a second address. Burning half of a broken stake means they lose half of it that way. But the doubters also win the faith pool, so a keeper who lets a vow fail and doubts it from a second address nets faith − stake / 2. Capping total faith at half of the stake makes that number zero or negative, so the trick never pays. Without the cap, a 0.1 GEN stake with 10 GEN of faith behind it would pay the keeper almost 10 GEN.

How judging works

Judging is a non-deterministic step. The leader validator reads the evidence and asks an LLM for a verdict. Every other validator repeats the reading and must agree on the verdict field only. The wording of the note may differ.

Verdicts

VerdictMeaning
FULFILLEDThe page shows the vow was completed as worded (and, when dates are visible, by the deadline). The vow becomes kept.
BROKENThe page is readable but does not show completion, shows the vow was not done, or counter-evidence gives concrete contradicting facts. The vow becomes broken.
UNCLEAROnly if the vow is too vague to judge at all. The vow becomes unclear and everyone is refunded.
UNREADABLEInternal result when the evidence page cannot be read. It is retried and is never stored as a final state.

Reading the page

  • A plain GET is used first, so HTTP errors are never treated as evidence.
  • If the page has little text (under 200 characters), a rendered read is tried as well and the longer text wins.
  • Only the first 300,000 characters are looked at, and markup is removed in a single left-to-right pass, so a hostile page cannot make validators run out of time. Only exact script, style and noscript blocks are dropped, comments are removed whole, and a lone < in the text is kept as text.
  • The judge sees the first 6,000 characters of the keeper's page and the first 2,000 of each counter-evidence page.

Unreadable pages

A page that cannot be read (network error, HTTP error, or fewer than 20 characters of text) does not decide the vow. judge can be called again an hour after the last attempt, up to three attempts, and the vow is then counted as broken. On the third and last attempt any text at all is judged, so a terse real page is not broken unread. The hour-long gap means a short outage or a rate limit cannot break a vow, while an unreachable page still cannot be used to dodge a verdict.

Evidence addresses

Evidence must be an http or https link to a named domain. These are rejected when the vow is made:

  • IP addresses, including four numeric labels as in 127.0.0.1.example.com
  • localhost, internal-looking and reserved top-level domains
  • public wildcard-DNS services such as nip.io
  • credentials in the link, whitespace and custom ports

Internationalized names are accepted in punycode form (xn--); the app converts them when you paste a link. This checks the shape of the name only. A domain someone controls can still point at a private address, so rely on the web module's own network rules for that.

Counter-evidence is a claim, not a fact

Pages from doubters go into the same prompt as the keeper's page, marked as untrusted. If a doubter's page gives concrete contradicting facts and the judge cannot tell that the keeper's page is right, the verdict is BROKEN: the keeper has to show the work. Pages that cannot be read are skipped, so a dead counter-evidence link never changes the outcome. When no counter-evidence exists the prompt is exactly the one used before this feature.

Why early judging needs a quiet vow

Skeptics can only react while a vow is open. Early confirmation therefore waits until half of the time has passed and is refused as soon as anyone has doubted the vow, so it cannot be used to close the door on a skeptic who is still looking.

Keeper records

record_of(address) returns kept and broken counts, the current streak, the best streak and kept_stake, the total GEN staked on kept vows. Vows can be made with a very small stake, so read the streak together with kept_stake.

Contract API

Amounts are in wei (1 GEN = 1018 wei). Views return JSON strings; large amounts are returned as decimal strings so they survive JSON. Deadlines are Unix timestamps in seconds.

Write methods

make_vow(text, evidence_url, deadline) payable

Creates a vow. The attached value is the stake.

textstringThe promise. Cleaned of control characters and angle brackets, at least 8 and at most 280 characters.
evidence_urlstringPublic http(s) link the validators will read.
deadlineintUnix seconds, between one minute and 366 days from now.

back(vow_id, side) payable

Adds backing to an open vow before its deadline. The attached value is the backing, at least 0.01 GEN. A keeper cannot back their own vow, and an address picks one side per vow. It can add to its position on that side later.

vow_idintIndex of the vow.
sidestring"faith" or "doubt". Total faith may not pass half of the keeper's stake.

challenge(vow_id, url)

A doubter submits one counter-evidence page before the deadline. See Vow lifecycle for how the three slots are ranked.

vow_idintIndex of the vow.
urlstringPublic http(s) link. It cannot repeat the keeper's page or another counter-evidence page.

pin_evidence(vow_id, url)

Keeper only, before the deadline, at most twice. The link must be an archive capture that already exists or a commit-pinned or content-addressed link.

judge(vow_id)

Asks the validators to rule. After the deadline it can be called by anyone, and again after an hour if the page was unreadable. Before the deadline it is an early check: allowed from the halfway point, only while nobody doubts the vow, and it can only confirm. A kept or broken result is proposed and opens a review window; unclear is final.

dispute(vow_id, url)

During a review window. Doubters dispute a kept proposal, the keeper and faith backers a broken one. One page per address.

finalize(vow_id)

After the review window, by anyone. Settles the proposal, re-judging once if pages were disputed. Records, embers and payouts change only here.

claim(vow_id)

Pays the caller what the verdict owes them. Each address can claim once per vow. The contract records the claim at once; the transfer itself is delivered when the claim transaction finalizes.

release(vow_id)

Closes a vow that has stayed open for thirty days past its deadline as unclear, so everyone can claim a full refund.

Frontend

A Vite and React app in frontend/. Each vow is a lantern in a canvas sky, with a ledger view, filters, keeper records and a details drawer.

Behavior worth knowing

  • Network time. The app reads the network date from the RPC endpoint when it can and uses it for deadlines and phases, so a device clock that is a few minutes off does not change what you see.
  • Vow links. Selecting a lantern updates the address bar to #vow=<id>, and the drawer has a Copy link button.
  • Wallets. Every installed EIP-6963 wallet shows up in a picker. The choice is remembered and reconnected on the next visit unless you disconnected on purpose. The app adds and switches to Studionet for you and follows account and network changes made inside the wallet. The panel shows the address, the GEN balance, your keeper record, the network status, Switch account and Disconnect.
  • Ledger. It can be searched and sorted. With a wallet connected, My vows dims every other keeper's lantern in the sky and filters the ledger.
  • Paging. The app loads the 60 newest vows and shows a Show older vows button when there are more.
  • Input. Stake fields accept a comma as decimal separator and non-Latin digits.
  • Evidence labels. Links are labelled as a public record, an archived snapshot or editable by its owner. This is a hint based on the shape of the address; it does not verify anything.
  • Motion. Animation respects prefers-reduced-motion.

Security and limits

Before staking real value. The wording of the rules in _prompt should be exercised on Studionet with real pages first. Counter-evidence widens the surface for prompt injection: a doubter can now try to steer the judge, just as a keeper always could.

Prompt injection

  • Vow text and page text are stripped of angle brackets and passed to the model as tagged data with an instruction to ignore commands inside them. This reduces prompt injection, it does not remove it.
  • Both keeper and counter-evidence pages are wrapped as untrusted data. The verdict is compared across validators, and a favourable verdict also needs a verbatim, dated quote that every validator finds on its own copy of the page, so a page that merely announces its own verdict cannot win.

Evidence and incentives

  • A keeper who controls the evidence page can write anything on it. Prefer pages the keeper cannot edit alone, such as a public repository, a commit history or a third-party listing.
  • The judge only sees the first 6,000 characters of text on the evidence page, so a quote has to come from there.
  • An editable page can still carry a backdated line. The check proves the page claims the work was done by the deadline, not that it was. The review window and firm records answer a lie: an archive capture from before the deadline that shows the page empty outweighs the page, so pin your own proof and capture suspicious pages before the deadline.
  • The contract cannot see which capture an archive link finally serves. Use the exact capture link; a link for a time with no capture may be answered with the nearest one.
  • A vow is read at judge, may be read again at finalize when pages were disputed, and is never reopened after that.
  • A fixed 0.01 GEN doubt can win a large share of a broken vow when nobody else doubts. That is intended, and it is why keepers should choose stakes they are willing to lose.
  • Anyone can fill the faith cap of a vow with a single backing, which stops others from adding faith. It costs the backer nothing if the vow is kept.
  • A doubter who stakes 0.01 GEN switches off early confirmation for that vow. It costs them the 0.01 GEN if the vow is kept.
  • Counter-evidence slots are ranked by each holder's current doubt. A keeper's second wallet can still occupy a slot; it takes a larger doubt to displace it.

Availability

  • release refunds everyone after thirty days, so an attacker who could keep judge failing would only delay a verdict, never lock funds. A keeper who manages that gets a refund instead of a verdict.
  • Every early check runs the judges, and the caller pays for it. The fifteen-minute gap limits repeats on one vow.

Testing

pip install -r requirements.txt
pytest

The suite runs the contract in memory with mocked web pages and LLM answers. It covers:

  • input validation, including the evidence address rules
  • backing rules and the faith cap
  • counter-evidence rules and slot ranking
  • early confirmation
  • a regression test for the self-doubt attack
  • every verdict and payout, and single-use claims
  • the unreadable-page retry path
  • streaks, paging and validator agreement

tests/direct/test_economics.py checks the payout math on its own: payouts never exceed the pool, and rounding dust stays at a few wei. test_hardening.py and test_economics.py need no VM.

tests/direct/test_adversarial.py holds the adversarial and end-to-end tests: manipulated evidence (invented quotes, undated or late work, prompt injection, validators that cannot find the leader's quote), post-deadline edits against firm records, coordinated counter-evidence and slot crowding, and complete stake, review, finalize and claim runs whose deposited, paid and embers totals must balance.

The Python test tools are pinned in requirements.txt.
